The Rising Cost of Malaysian Food

Food prices in Malaysia have risen sharply since 2020. Here is what changed, where it hit hardest, and what it means for your household.

Two decades of food prices

Malaysia's food prices stayed largely stable for a decade, until 2021, when global supply shocks changed everything.

Prices accelerated after 2020

Before 2020, food inflation held below 4% a year. The pandemic and subsidy cuts pushed the index past 140 in 2023, up 40% from 2010.

2008: Global food crisis
2020: COVID-19 pandemic
2022: Subsidy removal

Not only food inflated

Restaurants and hotels saw the steepest rises. The everyday grocery basket followed closely. Communication was the only category below overall inflation.

Where you live matters

Food costs vary widely across Malaysia. Sabah and Sarawak felt the sharpest rises, while Perak and Kedah stayed comparatively affordable.

Sabah had the highest food inflation

In June 2023, year-on-year food inflation ranged from 3% to 7% across states. Sabah and Sarawak led; W.P. Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya remained the most contained.

Sabah - highest at ~7%
Sarawak and Kelantan - above 5%
W.P. KL and Putrajaya - lowest at ~3%

Source: DOSM, June 2023

Eating out is getting further from reach

The gap between cooking at home and eating out has widened. This hits lower-income households hardest, regardless of state.

Rankings shift, but East Malaysia stays expensive

Selangor and Johor have consistently ranked highest for food prices. W.P. Kuala Lumpur has climbed in recent years, reflecting the urban cost-of-living squeeze in the capital.

Highlighted: Selangor, W.P. Kuala Lumpur, Sabah, Kelantan

How Malaysia compares in Southeast Asia

Malaysia sits in the middle of the regional pack, behind the Philippines, Indonesia and Myanmar, but ahead of Singapore, Thailand and Brunei.

Laos and Myanmar lead on food price levels

Laos recorded the highest average food Customer Price Index in 2022, driven by currency depreciation and import costs. Singapore and Brunei remain the most affordable. Malaysia's index of 121 sits near the regional median.

Laos and Myanmar - highest Customer Price Index
Philippines and Indonesia - above average
Singapore, Thailand and Brunei - lowest

Circle size = average food Customer Price Index (base 2015 = 100). Source: FAO, 2022
