The Rising Cost of Malaysian Food

Food prices in Malaysia have risen sharply since 2020. Here's what changed, where it hit hardest, and what it means for your household.

Two decades of food prices

Malaysia's food prices stayed largely stable for a decade — until 2021, when global supply shocks changed everything.

Between 2010 and 2020, food inflation stayed under 4% annually. The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted global supply chains, and the removal of government subsidies pushed prices to their highest sustained level in two decades. The food price index crossed 140 points in 2023 — meaning food costs 40% more on average than it did in 2010.

Not all food inflated equally

Restaurants and hotels saw the steepest price rises. Meanwhile, food and non-alcoholic drinks — the everyday grocery basket — rose nearly as fast. Communication was the only category to fall below overall inflation.

Where you live matters

Food costs are not the same across Malaysia. Selangor and Putrajaya saw the sharpest rises, while Perak and Kedah remained comparatively affordable.

📍 Malaysia is located in Southeast Asia, between latitudes 1°N and 7°N.

Eating out is getting further from reach

The gap between cooking at home and eating out has widened significantly — a trend that affects lower-income households the most. Wealthier states spend more in absolute terms, but poorer states feel the squeeze harder.

How Malaysia compares in Southeast Asia

Malaysia's food inflation sits in the middle of the regional pack — lower than the Philippines, Indonesia and Myanmar, but higher than Singapore, Thailand and Brunei.
