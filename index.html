The Rising Cost of Malaysian Food

Food prices in Malaysia have risen sharply since 2020. Here's what changed, where it hit hardest, and what it means for your household.

Two decades of food prices

Malaysia's food prices stayed largely stable for a decade — until 2021, when global supply shocks changed everything.

Between 2010 and 2020, food inflation stayed under 4% annually. The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted global supply chains, and the removal of government subsidies pushed prices to their highest sustained level in two decades.

Not all food inflated equally

Restaurants and hotels saw the steepest price rises. Meanwhile, food and non-alcoholic drinks — the everyday grocery basket — rose nearly as fast.

Where you live matters

Selangor and Putrajaya saw the sharpest rises, while Perak and Kedah remained comparatively affordable.

Eating out is getting further from reach

The gap between cooking at home and eating out has widened significantly — a trend that affects lower-income households the most.

How state food price rankings have shifted

Some states have climbed dramatically in the rankings — Selangor now consistently tops the list, while others have improved their relative position.

How Malaysia compares in Southeast Asia

Malaysia's food inflation sits in the middle of the regional pack — lower than the Philippines and Indonesia, but higher than Singapore and Thailand.
